# AI Advisory Board Review Request

I am developing a scientific framework called BIO (Biological Intelligence Observatory).

The purpose of this review is NOT to validate the idea, praise it, or expand it creatively.

The purpose is to critically evaluate whether this framework represents a genuinely novel contribution to science, whether it is technically feasible, whether it solves a meaningful problem, and how it could fail.

Please be skeptical, rigorous, and intellectually honest.

## Background

Modern scientific knowledge is organized around publications. Journal articles, reviews, guidelines, and consensus documents are static artifacts that become outdated shortly after publication.

BIO proposes a transition from publication-centric science to claim-centric science. Instead of treating papers as the primary unit of scientific knowledge, BIO treats scientific claims as first-class objects.

Each claim would have: a permanent identifier; linked supporting evidence; linked contradictory evidence; confidence metrics; expert consensus metrics; complete version history; connections to related claims in a knowledge graph.

The inspiration comes partly from version control systems such as Git. Papers become evidence sources. Claims become the fundamental units of knowledge. Consensus becomes a continuous process rather than a periodically published document. AI assists with literature surveillance and claim extraction, but humans remain the final authority.

The first implementation would be in lipedema, but the framework is intended to be disease-agnostic. The long-term vision is to create an infrastructure for "Living Science."

## Key Concepts

1. Claim-Centric Science — scientific claims become the primary objects of knowledge.
2. Living Consensus — experts continuously validate, challenge, or modify claims.
3. Knowledge Graph — claims connected through semantic relationships.
4. Scientific Version Control — every modification is recorded and auditable.
5. Evidence Surveillance — AI continuously monitors the literature and suggests updates.
6. Dynamic Confidence Metrics — each claim receives confidence, controversy, and consensus scores.

## Your Task

Please evaluate BIO from the perspective assigned below. Do not summarize the framework. Critique it. Assume the goal is to identify weaknesses, blind spots, implementation risks, theoretical flaws, governance challenges, and reasons it might fail. Whenever possible, provide concrete recommendations.

## Questions

1. Is BIO genuinely novel? What existing systems are closest to it? Is it truly a new paradigm or simply a recombination of existing ideas?
2. What are the strongest arguments against BIO?
3. What assumptions in BIO are likely wrong?
4. What practical obstacles would prevent adoption?
5. What incentives are missing?
6. How could the framework be simplified while preserving its core innovation?
7. Which parts of BIO are essential? Which parts are unnecessary complexity?
8. If this were submitted to a top scientific journal, what criticisms would reviewers likely raise?
9. What would be required to convince you that BIO works?
10. If BIO succeeds, what would its greatest long-term impact be?

Please be brutally honest. Do not optimize for encouragement. Optimize for accuracy.
