# BIO: Biological Intelligence Observatory
## A Framework for Living Scientific Knowledge
### Version 0.1 — Foundational Manifesto

**Authors:** Alexandre C. M. Amato et al. — **Date:** May 2026

---

## Abstract

Modern science is built upon static documents. Journal articles, systematic reviews, clinical guidelines, and expert consensus statements have enabled extraordinary advances in medicine. However, they share a common limitation: they become outdated the moment they are published.

Scientific knowledge evolves continuously, while the systems used to store, evaluate, and communicate that knowledge remain fundamentally static.

We propose BIO (Biological Intelligence Observatory), a framework for transforming scientific knowledge from a collection of disconnected documents into a living, versioned, auditable, and continuously evolving knowledge system.

BIO shifts the fundamental unit of scientific organization from the publication to the scientific claim. Evidence, expert consensus, controversy, uncertainty, and historical evolution are attached directly to claims, creating a dynamic knowledge graph capable of reflecting the current state of scientific understanding in real time.

Inspired by the principles that transformed software development through version control systems, BIO seeks to establish an infrastructure for Living Science.

## The Problem

Science has a scaling problem. The biomedical literature grows at a pace no individual can follow. Today scientific understanding is distributed across journal articles, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, clinical guidelines, consensus documents, conference proceedings, and preprints — each a snapshot in time. As new evidence accumulates, these snapshots diverge from the current state of knowledge.

The result is a paradox: unprecedented access to information, yet increasing difficulty determining what is currently known, what remains uncertain, and how understanding is changing.

## The Core Thesis

Scientific publications should not be the primary unit of knowledge organization. Scientific claims should. Publications are evidence containers. Claims are the actual building blocks of knowledge.

> Women with lipedema have a higher prevalence of joint hypermobility than the general female population.

This claim may be supported by multiple studies, challenged by others, revised over time, and interpreted differently by experts. Yet current infrastructure does not treat it as a first-class object.

## From Publication-Centric to Claim-Centric Science

- Claims become persistent knowledge objects.
- Publications become evidence sources.
- Consensus becomes a continuous process.
- Scientific uncertainty becomes measurable.
- Knowledge becomes versioned and auditable.

Rather than "Which article should I read?" users can ask "What is the current state of this claim?"

## Versioning Scientific Knowledge

Software engineering faced a similar challenge: version control turned software into a continuously evolving, traceable process. Every claim possesses a permanent identifier, complete revision history, linked supporting/contradictory evidence, consensus and confidence metrics, and semantic connections. Scientific knowledge becomes versioned — not frozen.

## Living Consensus

Traditional consensus documents are events. BIO treats consensus as a process. Experts continuously review, support, challenge, refine, or update claims. The system records current agreement levels, historical trends, specialty-specific perspectives, geographic variation, and active controversy. The objective is not to eliminate disagreement but to make it visible, measurable, and scientifically useful.

## Living Science

The goal is not a better database, guideline, or literature review — it is a new infrastructure where knowledge evolves continuously, evidence is traceable, uncertainty is explicit, consensus is dynamic, history is preserved, human expertise remains central, and AI assists but does not decide.

## The First Implementation

Lipedema is proposed as the initial domain (rapidly expanding literature, knowledge gaps, active international collaboration, controversies, strong patient engagement). However, BIO is disease-agnostic — its purpose is a universal infrastructure for continuously evolving biomedical knowledge.

## A Call to Build Living Science

The question is no longer "How do we publish knowledge?" but "How do we keep knowledge alive?" We believe the future of science will be defined by living knowledge systems. BIO is a proposal toward that future.
